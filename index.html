Firstlight

A physically based sky for three.js

How this works

The sky is the light source. One model of the atmosphere gives the sky colour, the sun's colour, the light bouncing in from the whole sky, and the blue haze on distant ridges. Move the sun and all four change together, which is why the scene stays believable at any hour.

Light loses a fraction of itself for every kilometre of air it crosses, so what survives is an exponential:

T= e−∫0dσt(s)ds
  • T: transmittance, the fraction of light that gets through (0 to 1, no unit)
  • σt: extinction, how much light air removes per kilometre (1/km)
  • d: path length through the air (km)

Real light units. Sunlight arrives at 133,800 lux above the air. The demo meters the light at the camera like a photographer's light meter and picks the exposure (EV) for it, so noon and dusk both look right. Switch Settings → Metering to Sunny 16 to see how a fixed camera would see dusk: nearly black.

Blue light scatters about 5.7× more than red (Rayleigh: σ ∝ λ−4, and (680/440)4 ≈ 5.7), so a low sun, seen through a long path, turns orange. The sky is precomputed into three small tables each frame, following Hillaire 2020.

Light is in lux and cd/m². Terrain colours are estimates; the atmosphere constants are Earth's, cited in the docs. The meter treats the RGB channels as luminance, which is approximate.
